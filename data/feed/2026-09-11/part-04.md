TEXT: One thing that scares me about coding with AI is that it's hard to feel your strategic mistakes

I.e. I STILL remember mistakes from my early career, in ~2018:

- using a complex form library instead of browser built-ins
- using a downright dumb webpack + PHP combo

These mistakes cost us valuable time. And when we rectified them, the tasks around them got faster.

But often, we didn't even see them as mistakes! We just thought "this is the way we do it".

We stuck to the tactical side of our job, and didn't consider high-level alternatives. Too many things to ship, too much pressure, too little interest in fixing it.

I see the same attitude in agents.

If you tell them to do a task, they will do that task. They aren't RL'd to think outside the box, or propose strategic alternatives.

So the human needs to be on the lookout for these strategic opportunities. But how do you do it, if you don't feel the pain of working in them?

I'm planning on building this into my /retro skill (still in-progress). That'll at least give the agent an opportunity to identify refactor opportunities. We'll see how it goes, but I'm still nervous about it.

TEXT: I can’t stress enough how little an idea matters compared to the agency of the people executing the idea. 

I have had the privilege of knowing and sometimes even working with some of the most successful people (by various metrics).

The difference between mediocre and excellent work and outcomes is predominantly one of agency. 

In practice this means: they dont wait for things to happen to them they go out and make things happen for them.

They don’t wait for someone else to do something, for someone to teach them, for someone to give them the path, etc. They just go out and find a way to do it.

I think the single biggest superpower these people have is the realization/belief that the world around them is completely mutable. Most everything that happens is because a person made it happen.

I used to tell people to look around the room you’re sitting in. Look at everything. Every noun. It almost all exists because a person willed it into existence. Nothing is stopping you from doing the same. 

I see people online all the time dismissing someone else’s success because “I had that idea first” or whatever. I mean… yeah? If so then the difference is… you. So a bit of a self own whenever I hear that.

Number one tip: act with agency.
