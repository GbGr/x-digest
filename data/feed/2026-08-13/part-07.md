I’m sharing how I came to be worried about this in my latest piece on AI & writing.

RT-OF @bojie_li (L1): Early 2025, Turing Books asked me if I were willing to translate @natolambert 's RLHF book if it gets published in 2026. I had the same thought with @natolambert that in 2026 AI would be writing books of publishable quality. I was wrong. Although AI has frontier intelligence in math and coding, it gets nowhere stronger in writing.

I tried Loop Engineering to write my open source book "AI Agents in Depth" https://t.co/6VBSv9Mete and it turned out to be worse after 100 iterations, and it never figured out the structural issues I feel evident. In terms of Chinese writing: Kimi K3> Claude Opus 5 > GPT 5.6 Sol. Similarly, the multimodal capability of several frontier models also have regression. Models cannot automatically get better at tasks if engineers in frontier labs do not target them.

Books and papers are important because they are compressed forms of knowledge. IMHO books and papers may be more important than code today. Today reproducing a paper is just one prompt + several days of GPU runs. Compared to re-using the code, I prefer reimplementing from the paper.

We will see whether foundational models will ever reach super-human in all fields including interaction, writing, multimodal, and even emotional capabilities, or it will be always target frontier intelligence for science and technology.
