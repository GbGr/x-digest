TEXT: When evaling models, we anchor on the median task. But this is like how devs estimate the median task accurately but underestimate the mean which tends to be ~2x estimated (thus "double your estimates").

This applies to models too—we optimize for time/cost on median tasks. But it's the tail tasks that determine a project's completion, and reliable models (like Fable and Opus) are the diff between success and failure.

Work with models as collaborators, not tools. Go beyond pair-programming, where given a narrow spec, the tool just executes in minutes. Instead, explain your intent and what success looks like, and let your collaborator explore, research, push back, go the extra mile, define specs, execute, verify, over hours/days while you observe and provide guidance if needed.

(See @bernhardsson's classic on why engineers are bad at estimation: https://t.co/lZBpsDhLvI)
QUOTED @Steve_Yegge: Fable is careful. None of the other models are careful. GPT-5.6 Sol, Opus, Kimi,  Grok. You can compare them all day long on capabilities, and it doesn't matter, because in order to use a model for real production work, it must first and foremost be careful.

That's the only dimension that matters to me. And if you're doing real production-facing customer-facing work with AI, it's the only thing that should matter to you, too. The other models will not be truly competitive at anything but proofs
